ResilioChain, Explained Simply

A smart assistant that finds you a new supplier the moment something goes wrong — written for people who don't write code.

What is ResilioChain? (in one breath)

Imagine you run a company that builds things — say, cars. To build a car you need thousands of little parts, and you buy those parts from other companies. When something goes wrong and your parts can't arrive, your whole factory can grind to a halt.

ResilioChain watches for trouble and, the moment a part stops flowing, quickly finds you a new place to buy it from — one that's safe, allowed, and reliable.

Think of it like a GPS that reroutes you around a traffic jam. Except instead of roads, it reroutes your suppliers.

Let's learn three words first

🏭
A "supplier" is a company that sells you something you need. If you make cars, a supplier might make the tiny computer chips inside the dashboard. No chips → no dashboards → no cars.
🌀
A "disruption" is anything that suddenly stops a supplier from delivering: a typhoon, a closed port, a war, or sanctions (when governments say "you're not allowed to trade with that company anymore").
💧
The "ripple effect" is how one problem spreads to hurt lots of things downstream — like dropping a stone in a pond.

The sneaky problem most people miss 🤔

A supplier can have your parts sitting right there in the warehouse — and you still can't get them.

How? Picture this. Your supplier has 48,200 computer chips ready to ship. They're boxed up. They're paid for. But the port is closed because a typhoon hit. The truck can't drive onto the ship. The ship can't leave the harbor.

The stock exists. The delivery is stuck. Having the parts and getting the parts are two completely different things. That's why ResilioChain doesn't just ask "who has stock?" — it asks "who can actually deliver?"

Our story: Typhoon Yagi 🌀

Typhoon Yagi closes the Port of Tanjung Pelepas, Malaysia for 72 hours. Our main supplier, SUP-001 (Chang Electronics), has 48,200 units of special automotive computer chips stuck behind that closed port. Twenty-three of our orders are about to be late.

Watch how ResilioChain handles it, step by step.

The five helpers working together

ResilioChain isn't one robot — it's a little team of five AI helpers, each with one job. They pass the problem down the line like a relay race.

🧠

1. The Supervisor — "the brain"

Reads the alarm, figures out how bad it is and who's affected, and sends the team to work. At the end, it writes the final recommendation.

💥

2. The Impact Assessor — "who gets hurt?"

The ripple-effect helper. It traces every order tied to the broken supplier and adds it all up: 23 orders, 48,200 units at risk.

🔎

3. The Sourcing Agent — "find a replacement"

Goes shopping for backups that make the same kind of part — and only ones big enough to actually cover all 48,200 units.

✅

4. The Compliance Agent — "is this one allowed & ethical?"

Checks sanctions, worker treatment, and environmental record (the "ESG score"). If a candidate fails, it sends it back to Helper 3 — up to 3 tries.

📋

5. The Mitigation Planner — "write the final answer"

Picks the best supplier that passed, works out extra cost and delay, gives a confidence score, and writes a clear one-page brief.

Why a supplier gets rejected 🚫

The sourcing helper found backups — but not all of them were good citizens.

🚫
SUP-FAIL (Vietnam) — had the right parts and enough capacity, but failed the ethics check. Its ESG score was too low. Rejected. Cheap and fast isn't worth it if it isn't ethical or allowed.
✅
SUP-002 — "PT Maju Jaya" (Indonesia) — same parts, enough capacity, and passed every rule check. It won. The orders get rerouted here.

The point: ResilioChain refuses to recommend a supplier that breaks the rules — even one that otherwise looks great.

"Confidence score" & "human review" 🎯

When ResilioChain finishes, it grades its own answer from 0 to 100 — its confidence score. It's like a student saying "I'm 92% sure this is right."

0 — needs a human85 — confident100

85 or above: the plan is solid and can go ahead quickly.
Below 85, or a supplier with a shaky ethics score: the computer raises its hand and says "a human should look at this first." That's human review.

This is on purpose. When the computer isn't sure, it doesn't guess — it asks a person. You stay in control. And every decision is written down in an audit trail, so you can always see why a choice was made.

Try the demo yourself 🚀

You don't need to understand any code to watch it work. Click Copy on any step.

Step 1 — Set up your secret keys (copy the example settings file).
cp .env.example .env
Step 2 — Load the practice data (pretend suppliers and orders).
python -m app.db.seed
Step 3 — Run the Typhoon Yagi story from start to finish.
python -m app.agents.graph --demo
Step 4 — Open the dashboard in your browser.
npm run dev

Then visit http://localhost:3000

You'll watch the alert arrive, the 48,200 stuck units get spotted, the Vietnam supplier get rejected for ethics, and PT Maju Jaya (Indonesia) get recommended — with a confidence score and a tidy one-page brief.

The big idea, in one sentence

When the world throws a surprise at your supply chain, ResilioChain finds you a safe, allowed, and reliable backup before the surprise becomes a crisis — and tells you how sure it is, so you always know when to double-check.
